## Stack decision

Start with Markdown, a restricted spreadsheet, synthetic demo, cost ledger, and application event export. Skills accelerate drafting and checking materials; they do not replace user observation, buyer approval, service access, or engineering capacity. No StreetProof evidence shows an agent stack matching a large marketing team. Every customer integration below is unverified as installed.

P owns strategy and offers. Q manages user evidence and enablement. E+A provide the demo and correct data. With two people, combine roles as in [current state](03-current-state.md). Do not buy CRM, automation suites, or paid keyword tools simply to fill a stack diagram.

## Skills and tools by AARRR

| Stage | Available skills as work methods | Recommended initial tools | Future integration and gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | customer-research, sales-enablement, content-strategy, social, copywriting | Spreadsheet CRM, 15-account list, demo and two assets | Search Console after a domain exists; paid keyword/ads after need and budget are clear |

| Referral | referrals, co-marketing, copywriting | Source field, separate permission, approved summary | Link/affiliate tooling after volume and program policy support it |
| Revenue | pricing, revops, sales-enablement | Offers, invoice/payment ledger, cost spreadsheet | Billing integration after local needs, fees, tax, and reconciliation are tested |
| Cross-cutting | marketing-plan, marketing-ideas, antislop-copywriting, analytics | Bilingual docs, experiment log, source register | Dashboard after source data reconciles |

Skill names describe capabilities available in the planning session rather than installed automation. GA4, Ahrefs, Customer.io, Stripe, Typefully, Notion, and affiliate tools cannot be listed as active customer connections without inspection. Google Drive/GitHub connectors available to the agent also do not establish a StreetProof CRM or repository.

## Minimum tool selection

| Need | Initial choice | Alternative and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Small pipeline | Spreadsheet with account_id, stage, owner, next_step, source, dates | Dedicated CRM when two owners need access/reconciliation the sheet cannot provide |
| Product metrics | Server event export with tenant scoping into a controlled spreadsheet | Product analytics after event contracts and data needs are clear |
| Marketing traffic | Unnecessary for a local demo; Search Console/analytics after a real site exists | Do not place trackers on receipts, photos, or token pages |
| Service email | Server adapter with a tested provider; Resend is a candidate | Account, domain, region, cost, and deliverability remain untested; use manual sends first if sufficient |
| B2B payments | Approved invoices and transfer reconciliation | Subscription gateway after buyer payment methods are known |

Resend documentation requires verification of a sender-owned domain. This is a candidate's technical prerequisite rather than proof that StreetProof has a domain, deliverability, or account approval. [M-03: Resend verified domains](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/introduction)

Google Analytics prohibits sending PII. If selected, E tests URLs, titles, events, and UTMs for identity, precise locations, report content, and tokens. Redaction does not replace collection limits. [M-02: Google Analytics PII](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/6366371?hl=en)

## Proposed operating demonstration

The following is a proposed exercise, not observed history. In week one, Q creates three synthetic accounts/tickets with different pilot.prepared and org.onboarded dates. Synthetic timestamps demonstrate preparation before baseline, then org.onboarded only when limited live is ready after baseline and shadow; the exercise does not mark real organizations as onboarded. E exports events without media or identity. Q calculates Activation14 from org.onboarded, marks young accounts pending, reports TimeToLive and accounts not yet live since pilot.prepared, and links every number to a source row. P reconciles one offer to the cost ledger. If a non-engineer cannot explain both clocks, the denominator, and pending cases, fix the template before purchasing a dashboard.

Cap the exercise at two team hours with synthetic data. Acceptance means files open, counts reproduce, and the marketing export contains no tokens/PII. No integration is called operational before the exercise actually passes.

## Capacity and funding unlocks

| State | People and load | Tools/channels opened | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Now, funding unknown | 2-4 roles; six/ten marketing team hours per week | Manual sales, checklist, proof | Confirm named owners and available time |
| S1 available | Same roles; contractors only for bounded tasks | Travel, materials, minimal tools | Cost ledger controls spend; one pilot |
| S2 available and paid value | Consider specialists before permanent staff | One paid test and CRM if needed | Matured conversion, margin, capacity, runway |
| Year two, added funding | Marketing Lead/Manager if repeated need is clear | Add channels after initial proof | New plan rather than automatic unlock by round name |

Initial effort allocation is 60/25/15 as in the [outlook](10-outlook-12-months.md). Use 70/20/10 only after a channel works. Initial AARRR capacity weights: Acquisition 40%, Activation 30%, Retention 15%, Revenue 10%, Referral 5%. These are planning weights rather than hours beyond the cap; during the pilot P may shift Acquisition to Activation/Retention and record why.

## Internal strategy and external execution

P remains responsible for ICP, messages, price, and channels. Q owns user understanding. Once funds exist, contractors may edit recordings, design materials, or conduct a specific review with clear scope and cap. Do not outsource positioning decisions to an agency without an internal owner. Next, test the spreadsheet and synthetic events, then add tools only for demonstrated operating bottlenecks.
