## Retention decision

StreetProof has no observed usage or renewal cohort. Initially, ensure teams reuse the workflow when eligible work occurs rather than pursuing daily logins. A low-volume building is not automatically churned after one ticket-free week. Q must inspect workload and operating schedules before sending reactivation messages.

Week-four operational retention means an activated account again uses triage through verification during days 22-28 after first activation. Report both all activated accounts whose window has matured and the subset with at least one eligible ticket in that window. Do not remove low-volume accounts from the overall report; label them as having no work opportunity. This is an early operating measure rather than contractual logo retention.

## Service mechanisms

| Moment | Action | Owner and tool | Cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| After first workflow | Debrief what helped and what added burden | Q, assistance log and permissioned user notes | One 20-minute session; no automatic testimonial request |
| Weekly pilot review | Inspect stalled tickets, rejected evidence, and corrections | Q+A with operator; event export and spreadsheet | 30 minutes/site/week; stop if it becomes unused duplicate entry |
| Waiting for access/parts | Show reason and follow-up owner | Customer operator; application status | Do not send reminders demanding false closure |
| No activity | Establish whether there are no tickets, broken access, or abandoned workflow | Q; agreed contact route | At most two follow-ups in 14 days, then record preference |
| Near pilot/contract end | Review results, service cost, export, and continuation options | P+Q; evaluation report and offer | Make decline/exit options clear |

Use emails, churn-prevention, customer-research, and copy-editing skills to design materials. Initial tools can be transactional email or approved manual messages, a preference list, support log, and contract ledger. Email/CRM integrations are not verified as installed. Do not assume a skill can access customer accounts.

## Experiments and stop rules

RET-01 tests weekly review at one site across four eligible usage weeks. Q caps analysis at one hour and the session at 30 minutes per week. Leading signals are blockers with an owner and action, plus fewer repeated questions. If two consecutive reviews yield no action or add duplicate recording, switch to exception-based review. Interview findings are descriptive; do not claim that reviews cause retention from one site.

RET-02 tests a one-page guide for the most frequent blocker. Q creates it only after the same blocker occurs three times, spending at most two hours. Measure whether users finish unaided on the next three occurrences. If they still need the same help, repair the product or SOP before adding guides. RET-03 tests one win-back conversation after a fixable exit reason has been resolved. No automatic discount. P stops if the customer declines contact or the main problem remains unresolved.

RET-04 prepares export, optional exit reasons, and deletion requests before the pilot. Q caps checklist drafting at one hour; E checks export/deletion promises against actual capability. If the route cannot operate, hold real-data onboarding until an agreed process exists.

Support belongs in service cost and margin rather than being hidden as marketing. Record P/Q/E/A minutes per account, severity, and root cause. If one site consumes over two unplanned support hours per week for two weeks, P holds new sites until scope or product improves. This is a proposed capacity threshold, not a customer SLA.

## Commercial retention and offboarding

Paid logo retention uses cohorts that actually paid and reached renewal. Separate completed pilots, extended pilots, non-renewing customers, and dormant accounts. NRR requires the same cohort's starting revenue, expansion, contraction, and churn; report N/A without a starting recurring revenue base. Do not count pilot fees as MRR.

On exit, Q requests an optional reason, provides the agreed export route, and sends deletion requests to the data owner. Media, metadata, and backup deletion follow contracted retention and actual recovery limits. Marketing promises cannot exceed product capability. Customers may decline publication even after a successful pilot.

## Execution sequence

The first 90 days produce a blocker log and evaluation of one pilot if it runs. Months 4-6 inspect repeatability at a second account and service cost. Months 7-12 consider segmented lifecycle only if roles and event patterns are stable enough. Q owns retention operations, P decides account capacity, and E+A repair causes. [Measurement](13-measurement-and-governance.md) defines matured denominators, while [Revenue](08-revenue.md) covers renewal and expansion.
