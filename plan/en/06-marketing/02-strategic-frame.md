## Recommended position

StreetProof's proposed position is evidence intake and verified work close-out for small facility teams. Start with Indonesian commercial building operators managing 1-5 sites, then limit the pilot to one site and 2-4 non-emergency corrective maintenance categories. Teams without a CMMS can test a standalone workflow. Teams with a CMMS can test StreetProof as an intake and evidence companion with an agreed export. No customer has validated this position.

Proposed buyer sentence: StreetProof helps operators turn photos and descriptions into ticket drafts, assign work, and ask a supervisor to inspect evidence before closing it. This describes the behavior the team intends to build. The team may call it available only after the demonstration and acceptance checks pass.

## Buyers and segment choice

| Role | Problem to establish | Evidence requested during discovery |
|---|---|---|
| Champion: facility/operations manager | Reports, owners, and proof are hard to trace | Recent workflow examples, volume, repeated copying |
| User: operator/helpdesk | Re-entering messages and repeatedly requesting locations | Active handling observations, beyond ticket elapsed time |
| User: technician/vendor | Evidence entry adds work | Upload time, device constraints, connectivity |
| Decision maker: operations head/owner | Benefit is unclear against change cost | Budget, signatory, conditions for a paid commitment |
| Approver: IT/data owner | Photo access, storage, export, deletion | Data owner, required controls, hosting boundaries |

Commercial buildings are the initial recommendation because a narrow corrective workflow and an operational buyer can be tested. This is a planning inference, not a survey finding about procurement speed. Campus is the fallback. A campus may become the primary segment if its sponsor, user access, data permission, and buying path are more concrete. Do not preserve the building segment merely because this document selected it.

Initial disqualifiers: seeking only a public complaint portal, requiring autonomous safety decisions, demanding ERP/CMMS replacement, or requiring custom integration before granting workflow access. Record justified exceptions; do not silently expand scope to win one logo.

## Problem size and frequency gate

Whether the problem is large and frequent remains unknown. The working hypothesis is repeated administration whose value depends on volume, wages, and closure quality. Request a two-week ticket count and observe a sample across categories. Separate time that staff can actually redeploy from cash savings that require changes to labor costs.

| Attractiveness factor | Hypothesis score 1-5 | Rationale and replacement evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Demand clarity | 2 | Research describes a problem; no purchase evidence |
| Budget availability | 2 | Buyer role is proposed; request approval process |
| Alternative weakness | 3 | Test the cost of the customer's messaging/spreadsheet process and CMMS |
| Buyer reachability | 3 | Test 15 named accounts rather than follower totals |
| Channel feasibility | 3 | Manual conversations permit a small time cap |
| Urgency | 2 | Ask about the last incident and the cost of delay |
| Proof availability | 4 | Baselines and ticket evidence can be inspected if permission exists |
| Execution risk | 2 | Data access and technician burden are untested |
| Speed to signal | 3 | Discovery may be quick; pilot evidence needs eight weeks |
| Team fit | 3 | Narrow scope fits the 2-4 person plan; skills remain unknown |

Hypothesis total: 27/50, low confidence. Validate first. This does not factually score the Indonesian market. No defensible TAM/SAM follows from the current building count or price evidence. Initial operational SOM is bounded by one-pilot capacity, rather than national market share. Once data exists, use two methods: genuinely eligible accounts multiplied by observed buying probability, and conversation/pilot capacity multiplied by observed conversion. Do not equate search volume with buyers.

## Business model and voice

Test a per-site subscription with no per-reporter charge. The Rp1,500,000/site/month price and Rp5,000,000 eight-week pilot fee are offer simulations. Setup and custom integrations need separate scope. [Revenue](08-revenue.md) shows when time savings alone fail to cover the price.

Use operational language people can check: AI draft, operator correction, duplicate candidate, required evidence, and supervisor approval. Avoid accuracy, savings, security, compliance, or integration claims without accompanying results and limits. Use specific invitations such as Review one ticket workflow or Discuss readiness for an eight-week pilot. Demo photos must be synthetic or permissioned; customer stories require separate publication permission.

## Reusable assets

Prioritize one complete workflow demonstration, a pilot readiness sheet, and a measurement template. P can reuse the same assets in conversations, onboarding, evaluation, and proposals. Public content follows repeated buyer questions rather than a volume calendar. Google recommends content for readers' needs; this does not establish StreetProof demand or guarantee rankings.
