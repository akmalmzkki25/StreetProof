# Segment comparison

Date: 2026-10-20 | Status: Proposed / research-based | Document ID: R-04

[English / Bahasa Indonesia](../../id/01-research/segment-comparison.md)

## Proposed choice

Start with an Indonesian commercial building operator running 1-5 sites, with a small facility team and repeated non-emergency corrective reports. Pilot one site. Keep campuses as the alternative when a named sponsor can grant access, a baseline and a buying route. This is an analyst recommendation, not a survey finding or evidence of customer access.

## Hypothesis scoring

Score 1 means weak feasibility; 5 means strong feasibility. Higher procurement score means easier purchasing. Scores encode assumptions; weights sum to 100. Weighted score = sum(weight × score)/100.

| Criterion | Weight | Commercial | Campus | Hypothesis behind difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Named buying authority | 20 | 4 | 3 | A site/portfolio manager may control a small operating budget; campus approval may span units |
| Recurring eligible volume | 20 | 4 | 4 | Both may have repeat corrective work; actual volume unknown |
| Pilot access and observation | 20 | 3 | 3 | Neither segment has confirmed access |
| Procurement feasibility | 15 | 4 | 2 | Private operating purchases may be simpler; no assumed campus procurement rule |
| Repeatable workflow | 15 | 4 | 3 | A bounded site/category set may standardize better than mixed faculty processes |
| Learning and reference potential | 10 | 3 | 4 | A campus can offer diverse reporters and a visible demonstration |
| Weighted score /5 | 100 | 3.70 | 3.15 | Analyst hypothesis only |

Sensitivity matters more than the initial ranking. Raising campus access from 3 to 5 adds 0.40; raising its buying authority from 3 to 4 adds 0.20. Campus reaches 3.75 and overtakes commercial at 3.70. A committed campus sponsor can therefore reverse the choice without changing the product thesis.

## Who participates and pays

| Role | Commercial hypothesis | Campus hypothesis | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reporter | Tenant, receptionist, security or staff | Student, staff, security or faculty administrator | Actual reporting paths and permissions |
| Operator | Facility coordinator | Facilities/service desk coordinator | Ten recent reports and active handling baseline |
| Technician | Internal or contracted worker | Internal or contracted worker | Mobile access, proof burden, access restrictions |
| Supervisor | Facility/operations manager | Head of facilities or delegated supervisor | Authority to accept or reject completion |
| Economic buyer | Owner/operator or portfolio budget holder | Authorized institutional budget holder | Named person, amount, approval route, timing |
| Data owner | Customer-appointed representative | Authorized institution representative | Written scope for reports, media, retention and export |

These are roles to discover, not asserted organization charts. Technical enthusiasm from a student or technician does not establish buying authority.

## Entry gates and exclusions

Require a supervisor, 2-4 agreed categories, a data owner, a baseline and a route to a paid decision. Exclude emergency dispatch, public city responsibility, mission-critical control and a site demanding a full ERP replacement. A low-volume site can test usability but may not test financial value. Prefer access and data quality over a prestigious logo.
