## Relevance

Limble directly challenges the proposed intake and duplicate-handling thesis. The reviewed official comparison table explicitly advertises an AI duplicate work-request prevention check in Enterprise. It is unsafe to describe duplicate detection as an unserved category.

## Evidence reviewed

| Area | Reviewed vendor claim and limit |
| --- | --- |
| Intake | Work-request portal, unlimited requesters, QR codes, photo capture/markup and request history are listed. |
| Approval | Review/approve requests and custom portal processes are listed in Premium+; custom approval processes in Enterprise. |
| Duplicates | AI duplicate prevention and request geolocation are listed in Enterprise. Candidate explanation, merge, unlink and retained-report semantics remain untested. |
| Maintenance depth | Work orders, preventive maintenance, asset records, reporting, offline and API vary by plan. |
| Unverified | Indonesian support, local billing, realistic configuration time and evidence acceptance workflow. |

## Comparable test

Submit two reports about one leak and a similar-looking leak in another room. Observe the duplicate warning, false matches, reviewer override and reporter histories. Reject completion lacking the category's evidence, then measure how the configured workflow handles resubmission.

## Implication for StreetProof

StreetProof can test explicit human linking and a smaller operational footprint, but neither is a proven advantage over Limble. If the buyer needs preventive schedules, parts and asset history immediately, a full CMMS may fit better. Keep a comparison of total effort and paid plan, not just feature names.
