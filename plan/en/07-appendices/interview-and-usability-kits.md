## How to use

These are evidence-collection instruments rather than research results. P recruits through authorized access; Q obtains note-taking permission, explains purpose and permits withdrawal. Do not record unnecessary identities or sensitive tickets. Separate interview, example-ticket and quote-publication permissions. Code participants P01, P02 and onward within a study rather than name them.

Start with 5 operators and 5 technicians as proposed recruitment targets from OR-01 §17.3, plus accessible buyers/approvers. These numbers support early learning rather than representative sampling. If only 3 participants are available, report 3; do not infer market percentages.

## Opening script

Explain that the team is studying facility-repair processes and has not promised savings. Ask to see the latest case that can be shared. State that answers will not penalize workers. Confirm duration, note types, retention, access and withdrawal under the agreed procedure.

## Operator and supervisor guide

1. Show the latest report from receipt to assignment. What information had to be requested again?
2. Which channels were used for that case and how often was data re-entered?
3. How were owner and priority chosen? Show a wrong-team example if available.
4. What made work wait: location, access, parts, budget or people?
5. Show adequate closure proof and an example of rejected proof.
6. How does the reporter receive status? What happens when a ticket closes before the problem is resolved?
7. Which monthly reports are read, by whom and for what decisions?

## Technician and vendor guide

Ask participants to describe real work, received information, finding the location/asset and documentation steps. Time input separately from physical repair. Ask which steps could be removed, network conditions, evidence obstacles, scope changes and who can accept work. Avoid leading questions about whether AI would help.

## Buyer, IT and procurement guide

Ask about current process costs and their basis, budget owner, systems that cannot be replaced, approvals, buying calendar, hosting/data requirements and reasons to reject a pilot. Once the problem is understood, present scope and the price hypothesis explicitly. Record objections and the next buying step. Interest, LOI, signed contract and payment are distinct statuses.

## Timing observation sheet

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Study/case ID | Study code and minimized ticket reference |
| Role/category/difficulty | Matching strata rather than personal identity |
| Active start/end | One operator task interval; record pauses and reasons |
| Task | Reading, re-entry, clarification, routing, proof review |
| Queue time | Recorded separately, excluded from active effort |
| Outcome | Success, assistance, failure, unresolved |
| Conditions | Network, parts, access, other system, SOP change |
| Record correction | Who changed the observation and why |

## Usability scenarios and rubric

| Task | Test data | Observed criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Submit report | Permissioned photo missing location | User finds the location field and receives receipt |
| Correct draft | Deliberately wrong AI category in synthetic data | Operator compares sources and saves correction |
| Assess duplicate | Similar locations with different problems | Operator rejects candidate without losing reports |
| Submit completion | Failed after-photo upload | Technician understands pending and ticket stays open |
| Reject evidence | Proof of a different object | Supervisor gives a reason and requests correction |
| View status | Valid, invalid and expired tokens | Reporter gets correct information without internal data |

Q records completion, time, errors, assistance and comments. Do not guide button choices before an attempt. Separate confusing language, missing steps, incorrect permissions and device limits. Review notes with participants when interpretation is uncertain.

## Synthesis and decisions

Use a finding table: ID, role, observed event, source, within-sample frequency, impact, confidence, causal hypothesis, action and owner. One quotation does not establish universal demand. P brings findings into the decision register; Q schedules retests after important changes. This template pre-populates no participants or outcomes.
